# Slide patterns

Choose the smallest pattern that communicates the page’s message.

| Pattern | Use for | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial cover | Opening idea | Centered or left title, short subtitle, one motif |
| Human cover | Event or community story | 45/55 title and replaceable photo |
| Section divider | Chapter transition | Large number, short title, one edge motif |
| Statement | One strong idea | Large editorial statement and up to three prompts |
| Process | Sequential work | Three to five numbered steps with one connector |
| Principles | Related concepts | Three spacious cards with concise descriptions |
| Human story | Quote or case | 55/45 photo and quote split |
| Comparison | Before/after or A/B | Equal columns and concise repeated rows |
| Activity | Workshop instruction | Centered task, timer badge, three to five steps |
| Takeaways | Closing synthesis | Three numbered takeaways |
| Next steps | CTA | Centered message, CTA block, contact or QR frame |
| Data point | One important metric | One large number, context line, small source |

## Pattern rules

- Use one message per page.
- Use repeated card dimensions and spacing within a page.
- Avoid placing paragraph text inside decorative illustrations.
- Use hierarchy, whitespace, and alignment before adding more decoration.
- Keep critical content editable; never bake the title or body copy into an
  image.
- When one page needs more than three distinct content regions, split it.

## Deck rhythm

For an 8–12 page deck, use:

1. Cover.
2. Context or problem.
3. One section transition when useful.
4. Two to five content pages.
5. One applied example, comparison, or activity.
6. Takeaways.
7. Next step or CTA.

Do not force every pattern into one deck.
